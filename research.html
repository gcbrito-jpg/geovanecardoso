Research

My research lies at the intersection of differential geometry and analysis. I am interested in how the geometry of a manifold, the structure of a vector bundle, and the presence of symmetries influence differential operators and their spectra. More recently, these questions have led me to optimal transport and the geometry of spaces of probability measures.

My doctoral thesis concerns the connection Laplacian on vector bundles over compact Riemannian manifolds. A central question is whether eigenvalue multiplicities persist under perturbations, or whether they can be removed by changing the Riemannian metric on the base manifold and the compatible connection on the bundle. In this setting, generic simplicity asks whether nonzero eigenvalues are simple for a residual set of geometric parameters.

To investigate these questions, I study the behavior of eigenvalues along one-parameter families of metrics and connections, using perturbation and variational methods. This connects local information about geometric variations with global spectral properties. I am also interested in the relationship between the connection Laplacian on a vector bundle and the Laplace–Beltrami operator on an associated principal bundle, where group actions provide a natural language for understanding multiplicities and equivariant simplicity.

My current research focuses on the geometry of Wasserstein spaces.
